# TOPC House: candidate houses and costs in Pune

Desk research, 2026-11-22, for topc-social/topc-tasks#341

## In short

Independent villas and row houses of 2,000–5,000 sq ft in the seven target areas rent for ₹55,000 to ₹3,00,000 a month. Of the 18 listings below, the median is ₹97,500 and the cheapest is ₹55,000 (a 3 BHK, 2,500 sq ft in Konark Cottages, Viman Nagar). Per square foot, the best value is in Magarpatta's Acacia Gardens: 4 BHK, 4,500 sq ft, ₹90,000–92,000, about ₹20/sq ft. Koregaon Park runs about three times that per sq ft. Deposits are mostly 2–3 months' rent, and brokers in Pune usually take one month's rent. Running a daytime working house is a legal grey area. Maharashtra's planning rules (UDCPR-2020) allow a professional office in a residential unit only "for own purpose" up to 50 sq m of carpet area on narrow roads, but place no area cap on plots facing wider roads (12 m and up outside the congested core). MSEDCL's tariff rules bill offices in residential premises at the non-residential (commercial) rate. At 500+ units a month that commercial rate actually works out cheaper than the residential slabs. Running costs other than rent come to about ₹20,000–43,000 a month for 20 people. That is my estimate from the sourced unit prices below.

## Use rules: can a residential villa run as a daytime working house?

**Planning rules (Maharashtra, applies in PMC).** UDCPR-2020 is "applicable to all Planning Authorities and Regional Plan areas" ([UDCPR-2020 text](https://www.grihaindia.org/sites/default/files/pdf/Griha-incentives/udcpr-sanctioned.pdf), cover page). What it permits depends on how wide the road in front of the plot is:
- In Residential zone R-1 (plots on roads under 12 m outside the congested area), the only office-type use permitted is "Professional Offices in residential tenement for own purpose not exceeding carpet area of 50 sq.m. each" (Reg. 4.3 (vi)). "Customary home occupation" means work by household members "without employing hired labourer" (Reg. 4.3 (iv); definition 75). R-1 also permits "Information technology establishment (ITE) (pertaining to software only)" on plots fronting roads 9 m wide or more (Reg. 4.3 (xix)).
- In R-2 (plots on roads 12 m and wider outside the congested area), "All uses permissible in R-1 shall be permitted … without any restriction of area" and "All uses or mix uses may be permitted irrespective of restriction on floor or area", apart from listed exceptions (Reg. 4.4.1).
- What this means for us (my reading, not legal advice): a 20-person shared workspace isn't "for own purpose", so on an R-1 plot it doesn't fit cleanly. A villa facing a 12 m+ road (R-2) gives much more room. Check the plot's zone and road width with PMC or an architect before signing.

**Society bye-laws.** A Moneylife Foundation note on Maharashtra co-operative housing societies says a flat can be used commercially only if the society and municipal zoning allow it. It cites bye-law 165(a): a ₹5,000 penalty per financial year for using a flat commercially against the bye-laws, plus complaints to the deputy registrar, the co-operative court and the municipal ward office ([MLF, 13 Oct 2023](https://www.mlfoundation.in/chs-problems-solutions/misuse-of-residential-flats-for-commercial-purposes-and-encroachment/310.html)). A Moneylife column (January 2026, seen only in a search result; the page returned 403) says limited professional use is permissible but a flat run "as a regular office with five to 10 employees, frequent visitors, business signage" stops being incidental professional use. It adds that charging extra maintenance "does not make an otherwise illegal commercial use legal" ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/housing-society-problems-and-solutions-maintenance-fairness-commercial-misuse-and-nonactive-member-threats/79325.html)).

**News of societies acting.** I found no Pune news of a society stopping a co-working space. The closest case: Archana Sahakari Gruha Rachna Sanstha in Pune got an injunction against tuition classes and a balwadi run from a bungalow. The Maharashtra State Co-operative Appellate Court set the injunction aside, holding that nuisance must be proven and noting the bye-laws had no express ban ([Free Press Journal, 26 May 2026](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/pune-housing-society-row-co-operative-appellate-court-allows-tuition-classes-balwadi-to-continue-says-nuisance-must-be-proven)).

**Magarpatta City.** Not found: no published Magarpatta rule on commercial use of residential villas. A law firm's page says every Magarpatta occupant signs a Township Maintenance Agreement, separate from the lease ([Startup Solicitors](https://www.startupsolicitors.com/magarpatta-city.html), seen in a search result, not opened). The use clause would be in that agreement or in the lease. Ask Magarpatta Township Development & Construction Co. directly.

**Electricity tariff.** MERC's tariff order lists who gets the residential tariff (LT I(B)). It includes "Residential premises used by professionals like Teachers, Lawyers, Doctors, Engineers, Chartered Accountants/ Tax Consultants, etc., in furtherance of their professional activities, but not including, offices…". The non-residential tariff (LT II) covers "Offices, including Commercial Establishments" ([MERC Case 217 of 2024, MYT order, pp. 724–729](https://www.mahadiscom.in/consumer/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/MSEDCL-MYT-Order_Case_no_217-of-2024.pdf)). So a shared office in a villa should be billed at LT-II. MSEDCL can reclassify a meter it finds used that way (my inference; I found no Pune case).
