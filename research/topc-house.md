# TOPC House: do community work houses already exist?

For task topc-social/topc-tasks#52 and the Ideato idea "TOPC House": a working house for creatives, "work from home, away from home", with a community kitchen. Related idea: "work café buddies", work dates with one person that slowly grow into a group.
https://www.ideato.social/idea/2c05f36a-375b-49f7-8e97-7a43ec79dbef

Desk research, 20 Oct 2026. Every claim links to a page that was opened. Prices come from listings and old articles, so they can be out of date.

## In short

Social work spaces exist in India, but they're either events-led coworking (Pune, Mumbai) or travel workations and invite-only clubhouses (Goa, Bangalore). I found nothing in Pune that's a house you work from with a shared kitchen. The evidence on "co-working has no bonding" points both ways: members say they feel less isolated, but they talk to only a small share of the people around them.

## 4. Does co-working have "no bonding"?

Against Kamal's claim (members say they bond):
- Deskmag's 2011 Global Coworking Survey (1,500+ people, 52 countries): 74% knew all or many coworkers' names, 92% said their social circle grew "a lot", 86% said their isolation went down. ([Deskmag](https://www.deskmag.com/en/first-results-of-global-coworking-survey-171))
- A SmallBizLabs/Emergent Research survey of members: 87% meet other members for social reasons, 54% socialise after work or on weekends, 83% feel less lonely. No sample size given. ([CFN News](https://www.cfn.news/news/2018/1/2/coworking-is-not-about-workspace-its-about-feeling-less-lonely))
- HBR, 2015: coworkers rate their thriving close to 6 on a 7-point scale, at least a point above regular office workers. ([NoDesk summary of HBR](https://nodesk.co/articles/why-people-thrive-in-coworking-spaces/))

For it (the bonding is thin):
- Deskmag, 2018 survey: extroverted members talk to about 6 others, "only" 13% of the members present. Ambiverts talk to 4, about 18%. ([Deskmag](https://deskmag.com/en/coworking-spaces-for-introverts-extroverts-ambiverts-member-report-study-1014/2))
- Yardi Kube (a coworking software company), July 2026: cites a 2024 GCUC survey saying 84% feel more motivated working around others, then says "The catch: most of them never talk to anyone." That second line is the blog's own view, not a survey number. ([Yardi Kube](https://www.yardikube.com/?p=29393))
- Optix's 2024 survey of about 100 operators: 77% of community managers wish they had more time to connect with members. ([Optix](https://www.optixapp.com/?p=17814))

So the claim as stated ("no bonding") is too strong. People do feel less alone. But most of them know only a few people, and bonding depends on someone running it. Most of the positive numbers come from industry surveys of people who chose coworking, and they're old. I found no India-specific numbers.

"Café working is tacky" is a taste claim. I didn't look for evidence on it.

## What this means for TOPC House

- The gap is real but narrow. Pune has creative and events-led coworking (Ekattha), but no house with a shared kitchen where locals work every day. Elsewhere in India, the social places are travel workations or invite-only clubs (Zo House).
- Closest in India: The Hive (Mumbai) for the house feel, Zo House for living, working and eating together, Goa hostels for the community kitchen. Closest anywhere: Hoffice.
- TOPC could be different by being for locals rather than travellers, cooking and eating together every day instead of an events calendar, and growing from one-to-one work dates. Bonding comes from a ritual (shared goals, breaks, a meal), not from desks.
- Cheapest test, free: run Hoffice-style work dates in Pune. One person, one café or one home, a fixed 2–3 hours with a goal check at the start and the end. Post it on Luma or Nomads.com (Pune has no meetups there, so it'll stand out) and count who comes back a second and third time before spending a rupee on a house.
