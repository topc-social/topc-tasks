# Deadlines, not scarcity, pay honest developers

The best openings for one experienced full-stack developer with Claude are jobs where someone already earning money faces a deadline or a shut-off, and the fix is code or paperwork. Three lead the ranking: **fixed-price rescue of vibe-coded apps**, **Amazon seller suspension appeals** and **DPDP readiness packs for Indian companies**. The largest sums in the research (₹14 lakh to ₹1 crore for medical seats and study abroad) go to people promising outcomes they cannot deliver, so they do not show what anyone would pay an honest seller. The clearest queue markets (US and Schengen visa slots, Tatkal, Spanish appointments) mostly want queue-jumping, which breaks rules or law, and those directions are excluded here and recorded as findings. **No buyer has been interviewed for this report.** Nearly every price is seller-listed, many sources were seen only as search snippets, and the sharpest stories are single unverified reports. The ranking is my judgment, a list of what to test first and not proof of demand. No source tested whether anyone would pay an honest, no-guarantee version of any case, and no note holds a Pune-specific data point.

## Read the tags before trusting the ranking

The tags come from four separate research notes that used different labels, and I kept them. **[Spend]** means a charged price or reported bill, and vendor list prices are asking prices. **[Pain]** means complaints or stars, not money. **[Opened]**, **[read]** and **[fetched]** mean the researcher read the page. **[Summary only]**, **[snippet]** and **[digest]** mean only a search summary was seen. **[vendor]** or **[seller]** means the source sells something related, and **[old]** or **[older]** means before 2025 or undated. The business note carried its rule as a statement rather than per-claim tags, naming the pages opened in full and calling everything else digest-level, so its [opened] and [digest] labels are my direct rendering of that statement.

## Three cases show paid offers tied to a shut-off or deadline

**Rescue of vibe-coded apps is the only case with a visible ladder of paid offers from many sellers.** Audits are listed at **$199, $799 and $1,499**, and one rescuer quotes **$25K-$50K** for most rebuilds and up to $100K for complex SaaS, though he sells rescues himself and discloses no client count ([McKelvey](https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/vibe-coded-app-audit-cost)) [Spend: vendor asking prices] [Opened]. A second vendor lists triage at $500-$1,500 and row-level security patches at $2,500-$5,000, with its FAQ and body text disagreeing on some figures ([Afterbuild Labs](https://afterbuildlabs.com/resources/state-of-vibe-coding-2026)) [Opened]. Hourly rates conflict badly: Upwork reportedly shows $16-$35/hr ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/vibe-coding-developers/)) [Summary only], while 404 Media reported $200+/hr via secondary coverage in 2025 ([Slashdot](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/25/09/13/054206/the-software-engineers-paid-to-fix-vibe-coded-messes)) [Summary only]. The desperation evidence is risk rather than behaviour: a passive scan of 30,998 apps found **2,096 of 3,680 reachable Supabase apps allowed unauthenticated reads** and 1,332 shipped a secret, reported by a publisher that sells scanners and did not reproduce the results ([VibeEval](https://vibe-eval.com/updates/vibe-coding-security-monthly-aug-2026/)) [Pain, not spend] [Opened]. Implied buyers are founders with paying customers, enterprise security questionnaires or investor due diligence, and most exposed apps are probably hobby projects that will never pay (my reasoning, with the paying share unknown). Blockers are a dozen-plus named sellers, liability for security promises that was not researched, free detection from Lovable and Supabase ([McKelvey](https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/vibe-coded-app-audit-cost)), and agents getting better at the simple fixes. The opening I see is fixed-price sprints between the $199 audit and the $25K rebuild, which no independent sales data confirms.

**Amazon appeals are the cleanest case of people paying for a document under pressure.** Listed flat fees run **$1,500 to $2,300** at one law-branded seller, which also reports a competitor averaging about $1,495 ([AMZ Sellers Attorney](https://www.amazonsellers.attorney/best-amazon-appeal-services.html)) [digest] [seller], and **$3,500** at another ([Amazon Seller Lawyer](https://amazonsellerslawyer.com/blog/success/get-sellers-back-to-selling-on-amazon-after-suspension/)) [digest] [seller, exact page not confirmed]. A Fiverr provider lists 2,100 completed orders, which shows repeat demand but no price ([Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/u_5eaec9329390/reactivate-your-amazon-seller-account)) [digest]. Desperation is inferred from a stopped income, and Indian sellers do post suspensions publicly ([Seller Central India forum](https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/cc39ed48-84af-47e1-893d-82e3065b5bc5)) [digest]. No suspension count or India price was found. The blockers are outcome risk (no service can guarantee reinstatement, and appeals may be limited, per a [low-authority article](https://amazon-appeal.vercel.app/en/articles/amazon-account-reinstatement-service-cost)) and reputation damage from failed appeals. Forum rules must be read before any outreach there. No evidence exists on the success rate of AI-drafted appeals.

**DPDP has the widest price spread and a fixed date, but no enforcement yet.** Vendor quotes of **₹15 lakh to ₹2 crore** sit against in-house builds of under ₹50,000 to ₹8 lakh, and end-to-end consulting is listed at ₹1.5-7 lakh ([eCorpIT](https://ecorpit.com/dpdp-compliance-cost-indian-startups-2027-deadline/)) [opened] [seller]. Both ends come from sellers, the top band is identical for very different company sizes, and the article itself says no neutral pricing exists, so this is not a market price. Main obligations and penalties start **13 May 2027**, with ceilings up to ₹250 crore ([eCorpIT](https://ecorpit.com/dpdp-compliance-cost-indian-startups-2027-deadline/)) [opened], and that figure stays a fear number until the Board acts. One firm reads the law as covering every entity processing digital personal data ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/tag/digital-personal-data-protection-dpdp-rules/)) [digest]. Data mapping, notices, consent logs and breach runbooks suit one developer, though buyers may still want a law firm's opinion. This is urgency by calendar, not observed panic: no source shows a company scrambling.

## The next tier has real pain but a catch on price, channel or buyer

**Stolen AI keys produce the most acute loss per person, with the weakest proof.** A three-person Mexican startup reported **$82,314.44 in two days against about $180 monthly**, and Truffle Security found 2,863 live exposed Google keys; the bill claim comes from an anonymous Reddit post whose author had not answered The Register ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/03/03/dev-stunned-by-82k-gemini-api-key-bill-after-theft/4925029)) [Spend: reported bills, mostly unconfirmed] [Opened]. A $15K startup-ending bill is known only from a headline ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/usd15k-bill-destroyed-a-solo-developers-startup-how-hackers-are-using-leaked-google-api-keys-to-go-wild-with-gemini-ai-for-free)). Victims have little money, so this sells before the leak rather than after it, as a check and cap setup bundled into rescue. Providers could ship the same protection.

**Hacked and banned Meta accounts show the clearest genuine need with the worst price evidence.** The only detailed list is from Brazil in 2023: **200 to 5,000 reais**, and what buyers got was mostly process knowledge ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/hacked-instagram-account-recovery-ethical-hackers/)) [read, old, Brazil, not India]. It must not be read as an Indian price. A business with nearly a million followers was told it "cannot request another review" ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-ai-moderation-account-bans)) [read], the Oversight Board found bans "lack due process", and paying Meta Verified subscribers say promised support did not arrive, though the Board upheld the specific ban it reviewed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/04/metas-oversight-board-says-account-bans-lack-due-process-transparency/)) [read]. Blockers are serious: Meta authorises no outside service, free guides exist, ChatGPT performance was not tested, and an upfront fee from a locked-out person looks exactly like the scams Bitdefender describes ([Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-gb/blog/hotforsecurity/instagram-account-recovery-scams)) [read]. A safe version never asks for passwords or codes, claims no Meta contact, does not help accounts banned for real harm, and starts with a free diagnosis.

**GST is not a story of overpaying for the reply.** One CA firm lists **₹3,500 per reply and ₹17,000 per appeal or advanced reconciliation** [opened] [seller], and ICAI's recommended minimum is ₹30,000+ in Class A cities on a February 2020 scale [opened, older] ([Tax Garden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-notice-reply-consultant-fees-india-2026)). The fee is small next to the demand, and the costly part is the reconciliation behind it. No count of small-business notices exists in the notes. The channel is the CA, so selling to CAs looks shorter than selling against them (untested), and representation limits under CGST s.116 are unverified.

**Rental renewal is the largest routine payment and the thinnest proof.** One lease required about a month's rent per year on renewal ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rozg9b/snapshots/2026-03-13T20%3A53%3A37.763389Z)) [fetched], and another renter faced half a month to renew or **₹57,000** to move ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sqeh9x/snapshots/2026-04-24T21%3A42%3A55.690026Z)) [fetched]. A one-month norm is reported in Delhi, Mumbai and Bangalore ([Housing.com](https://housing.com/news/what-are-the-brokerage-charges-for-renting-a-flat-in-india)) [snippet, undated, vendor]. A legal Q&A says tenants are not bound to pay on renewal ([LawRato](https://lawrato.com/property-legal-advice/do-we-have-to-pay-brokerage-every-year-in-mumbai-india-243422)) [snippet, undated], but a signed clause may bind. These are two posts from a mirror that was not checked against the live threads, this is annoyance more than desperation, Maharashtra and Karnataka brokerage rules were not read, and NoBroker already sells plans listed at ₹1,649-₹5,999 plus GST ([NoBroker forum](https://www.nobroker.in/forum/what-is-the-difference-between-the-nobroker-tenant-plans/)) [snippet, vendor].

**App Store rejection has a plausible buyer and no price.** Apple reportedly blocked updates for Replit and Vibecode in March 2026, but that dispute concerns the builder platforms, not their users' apps ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/03/18/bad-vibes-apple-blocks-updates-for-some-ai-coding-apps-in-the-app-store)) [Summary only]. Blogs report rejections of user apps under guidelines 4.3, 2.1, 4.2 and 2.5.2, with unverified statistics ([Axon Build](https://axonbuild.com/blog/vibe-coded-app-rejected-app-store/)) [Summary only]. It needs iOS experience, Apple's calls are uneven, and it belongs as a rescue line item.

**Health claims have the strongest independent loss figure and an untested buyer.** Insurers rejected **₹26,037.65 crore in FY23-24** ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/health-insurance-claims-worth-rs2603765-crore-rejected-by-insurers-in-fy2324-govt/76282.html)) [digest, page returned 403], and in FY24-25 the Ombudsman logged 53,184 complaints with about **71% resolved for the policyholder** ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/life-insurance/irdai-cannot-explain-why-health-insurance-claims-go-unpaid-583814.aspx)) [digest]. The source note set this aside because it studied earning businesses and these buyers are policyholders. Your question covers people, so I ranked it. Rejected money is not all winnable, and the 71% describes cases that reached the Ombudsman, not all denials. There is no fee evidence at all, and whether drafting appeals for payment raises regulated-activity questions has not been checked.

**CBAM is the most exposed export case and the hardest to reach alone.** GTRI estimates exporters may need to cut prices **15-22%** ([GSTimes](https://www.gstimes.in/eus-cbam-to-hit-indian-steel-aluminium-exports-from-2026)) [opened, single estimate, 1 Jan 2026], and MSMEs lack plant-level emissions data. Certificate sales reportedly start 1 February 2027 ([Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/cbam-timeline-deadlines-phases-what-to-expect-2025-and-beyond)) [digest, vendor]. No consultant fees, MSME counts or documented lost orders were found, actual emissions need an accredited verifier, and reaching exporters through trade bodies is relationship selling.

## The remaining eligible cases are too thin, too crowded or too cheap

**Token cost audits** rest on one developer's **$11,922 Cursor bill**, which he concedes came from his own usage and who also sells tooling ([DEV](https://dev.to/bytedesk/i-spent-11922-on-cursor-in-under-4-weeks-heres-how-i-fixed-it-3gh2)) [Spend: one self-report] [Opened], and on Uber reportedly exhausting its 2026 budget in four months ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/lucia-auerbach/uber-blew-through-2026-ai-budget-in-four-months-now-it-is-capping-employee-use/91355199)) [Summary only]. Big buyers use vendors and internal caps, so this is consulting at best. **SOC 2 readiness** has conflicting prices, from ₹2-5 lakh ([TCSA](https://www.tcsa.in/frameworks/soc-2/cost-guide)) [opened] [seller] to ₹15-35 lakh ([CodeSecure](https://codesecure.pages.dev/blogs/soc-2-type-2-compliance-cost-india)) [digest] [vendor, attribution approximate]. "Deals stall without it" is stated without data, and only a CPA can issue the attestation. **API breakage monitoring** has a drift statistic relayed by a monitoring vendor ([FlareCanary](https://dev.to/flarecanary/41-of-apis-drift-within-30-days-what-the-data-says-about-api-reliability-bhi)) [Pain; no cost data] [Summary only] and no spending evidence, so a demand check inside your existing Breakwatch project beats new work.

**E-invoicing alerts** address a live rule: invoices not issued in the prescribed manner can hit the buyer's ITC ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1089104)) [opened], and the 30-day limit applies at ₹10 crore AATO and above ([GSTN advisory](https://www.mahagst.gov.in/public/uploads/gstnadvisory/1760531580_293%20Advisory%20Time%20Limit%20for%20Reporting%20e%20Invoice%20on%20the%20IRP%20Portal%20%20Lowering%20of%20Threshold%20to%20AATO%2010%20Crores%20and%20Above.pdf)) [digest]. The loss is unquantified, and incumbents such as Tally already cover it. **EUDR** has the nearest date, 30 December 2026 or 54 days away ([VinciWorks](https://vinciworks.com/blog/eudr-delayed-again-what-changed-what-is-now-fixed-and-what-businesses-should-do-in-2026/)) [digest] [vendor], but no cost data, a free Coffee Board app ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2025/12/04/europian-union-deforestation-law-puts-kerala-coffee-trade-alert-compliance-window-narrows.html)) [opened], and an EU importer who owns the filing.

**RTO and passport help** have honest room but small margins. Agents reportedly charge **₹1,200 against a ₹200 licence fee** in Bhubaneswar, while the minister disputes the failure of online services ([OdishaTV](https://odishatv.in/odisha/online-services-in-bhubaneswar-rtos-a-massive-failure-brokers-continue-to-exploit-public-10961769)) [fetched]. Lucknow passport touts asked ₹400-₹1,200 and claimed an inside connection ([Amar Ujala](https://www.amarujala.com/lucknow/touts-misleading-applicants-outside-passport-seva-kendra-in-lucknow-2026-08-02)) [fetched]. People appear to pay for reassurance and in-person help, which software replaces poorly (my reading). **Visa file preparation** is legal but does not touch the bottleneck: B1/B2 waits run 7.5 to 12 months, agent quotes of ₹40,000 and $4,000 are unverified, and the source is a vendor already selling this ([Visarun](https://visarun.ai/blog/us-b1b2-visa-appointment-slots-for-indians-2026)) [fetched, vendor].

**Pre-payment checks serve the most harmed people and show no revenue.** A Hyderabad graduate allegedly paid **₹3.2 lakh**, starting with ₹50,000 to schedule an interview that never happened ([Hyderabad Mail](https://hyderabadmail.com/hyderabad-job-seeker-cheated-fake-cognizant-placement-scam/)) [read], a Delhi racket allegedly took ₹3,000-₹4,000 per victim ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/delhi-cyber-police-bust-fake-placement-racket-seven-arrested-for-cheating-job-seekers--20260804130127)) [read], and a Patiala youth says he paid ₹14 lakh and was stranded at a blacklisted Russian university ([PTC News](https://www.ptcnews.tv/punjab-2/punjab-youth-duped-of-rs-14-lakh-admitted-to-blacklisted-russian-university-agents-booked-4419901)) [read, details conflict slightly]. A Delhi businessman allegedly paid ₹32 lakh in stages for an MD seat that never came ([Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/state-news/delhi/delhi-businessman-allegedly-duped-of-rs-32-lakh-over-promise-of-md-radiology-seat-at-dy-patil-medical-college-pune-accused-booked-173320)) [read]. Repeated payment after the first promise slipped is the strongest desperation signal in the whole research, but these are single complaints, not court findings. National cyber-fraud totals have no job-fraud breakdown that the research could find. Families pay for the promised outcome, and a checker that says "no one can sell you this" cannot charge like one that promises the seat. Free ChatGPT also covers much of it, reliable blacklists were not confirmed to exist, and these checks read as free public goods.

## Conclusion

The research splits desperate spending into three markets. Where supply is truly scarce, what people want is forbidden. Where people chase a life outcome, the money goes to whoever promises it, and an honest seller cannot make that promise. Where the rules are merely hard, and the buyer has revenue and a clock, a fixed deliverable can be sold legitimately, and this is the only market that fits one developer with no capital. So the brief's words "overpaying" and "queuing" point at the wrong places, and "a working business that just stopped" points at the right one. Common to the top cases is a product shape, as my inference: a flat price for a stated deliverable, never for the outcome. Dollar-priced rescue and Amazon work also carry more per job than rupee-priced GST or RTO help.

The next evidence has to come from sales, not more reading. The notes' sharpest tests are one fixed-price rescue, one flat-fee Amazon appeal for a real suspended seller, and a DPDP pack offered to five Indian SMEs, in that order of what I would try. Any one of them would be stronger evidence than everything above, because it would be the first time a buyer has actually been asked. Reach is unproven: no acquisition data exists for any channel, and Reddit posting is closed to you.
