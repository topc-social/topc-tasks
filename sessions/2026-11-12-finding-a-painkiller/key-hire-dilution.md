# Giving a key person real stake when partners won't dilute

Desk memo for topc-social/topc-tasks#104, 2026-11-06. Trigger: at Billmobi the sales head needed 7-8%, nobody would dilute, and 1% plus all the revenue cash wasn't enough. Question: could this be a step zero for TOPC?

**Read: weak as a product on its own.** The problem is real but cheap fixes exist, they only work before the fight, and nothing here shows anyone paying. No founder was interviewed, so demand is untested.

## What was found

1. **Founder conflict is commonly cited as a top killer, but the number is loose.** Wasserman's *The Founder's Dilemmas* is the source of "65% of high-potential startups fail from co-founder conflict". Later articles drop "high-potential". [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/starting-a-business/ask-a-startup-lawyer-how-should-you-manage-co-founder/375248), [JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/how-to-split-equity-between-co-founders-8795115). The search found no published method behind it, and it covers all conflict, not dilution deadlocks.
2. **Splits get fixed very early.** 73% of founders in Wasserman's survey split equity in the first month. [The Crimson](https://thecrimson.com/article/2012/4/9/hbs-start-noam-founder). Survey of US tech and life-science founders.
3. **7-8% is far above normal for a hire.** Carta's median for a first hire is about 1.5%, falling to about 0.33% by the fifth. [Carta benchmarks](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-first-hire-equity-benchmarks-by-sector/), [SaaStr on Carta data](https://www.saastr.com/how-much-equity-to-give-your-first-employees-the-real-data-from-50000-startups). After raising money, VP-level hires commonly get 1-2%, but a commenter warns this fails for someone who is really a co-founder. [David Cummings](https://davidcummings.org/2014/05/06/minimize-cofounders-and-save-equity-for-key-hires/). US data, mostly venture-track; Carta's pages blocked direct reading, so these figures come from search summaries.
4. **The standard fix is a pool set aside up front.** Pre-seed pools start near 10%, seed medians run about 10-14.5%. [Carta pools](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-option-pool-startup-size-benchmarks/). Same caveat as above. It's free apart from legal paperwork, and it's the answer lawyers already give.
5. **Once someone refuses, the options mostly need their consent.** Buyback only works if the holder is willing, and restructuring needs their cooperation. Issuing new equity to the team is the one route the article doesn't tie to their consent, though investors may need to approve it. [Perkins Coie](https://legacy.perkinscoie.com/insights/blog/dealing-dead-equity). Written about departed holders, so a close match, not an exact one.
6. **Dynamic splits exist, with little independent evidence.** Slicing Pie moves shares with contribution. Its creator claims such companies do better without citing a study, in [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/how-split-equity-without-drawing-blood), an article adapted from his own book. No independent adoption or outcome data turned up. Reported limits: record keeping, every partner must commit, and it doesn't fit fixed-percentage investors. [XB Software](https://xbsoftware.com/blog/split-equity-in-a-startup/).
7. **India adds limits.** Pool size has no legal cap, but raising it needs a special resolution and an SH-7 filing. [Treelife](https://treelife.in/startups/esop-pool-creation-for-startups/). Sweat equity (section 54) goes to employees and directors, and outside advisors usually need sweat equity or phantom arrangements under a separate scheme. [EquityList](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/sweat-equity-shares). These are firm and vendor blogs, not the statute. Check with a CA.

## What this means for the Billmobi story

- The sales head's 1% was close to the going rate for a hire, while 7-8% is a co-founder's ask. So he may have been a late co-founder being offered a hire's deal. Billmobi's stage and whether it had a pool aren't known here, and US benchmarks may not fit it.
- A tool can't win once partners have said no. It would have to be set up on day one, before anyone feels the pain, and people rarely pay to prevent a fight they aren't having.

## What would change this read

- **For:** 3 of 5 founders describe losing a specific person over this in the last 12 months and have tried something to fix it.
- **Against:** founders say a pool solved it, or the person left for reasons other than stake.

## Not found

No data on how often a key hire is lost because partners won't dilute. No Indian numbers. No reliable first-sales-hire benchmark at seed.
